From understanding the mechanisms to imagining new ones.

I'm a PhD student in Computer Science at The University of Texas at Austin. My research lives at the intersection of statistical learning theory and large-scale experimentation. I'm especially interested in how data, architecture, and learning algorithms interact to shape model capabilities, generalization, sample efficiency, and computational cost.

At the model level, I study Mixture-of-Experts architectures, sparse and hybrid attention, and long-context modeling. My work asks why certain expert structures are easier to learn, how data–architecture interactions shape specialization and sample complexity, and how models can retain useful information while allocating computation more effectively.

Alongside theoretical work, I have hands-on experience with large-scale pretraining of sparse MoE language models across hundreds of GPUs. I've worked on hybrid sparse and compressed attention, long-context scaling, and attention dynamics, studying how architectural choices affect training quality, memory use, and compute. I also study learning from feedback through RLHF and preference optimization.

I'm increasingly drawn to what happens beyond the model: how tools, persistent memory, and structured world knowledge can help agents explore, gather evidence, and reason over long horizons. Looking ahead, I'm excited to explore agentic learning, adaptive inference, and self-improving systems—not only to make AI more efficient, but to discover new ways for it to learn, adapt, and act.
